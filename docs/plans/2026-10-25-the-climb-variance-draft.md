# The Climb: levers for variance

**DRAFT 2026-10-25, for the user's call.** The user: "my main drag for the
Climb is that it feels almost a little formulaic and there's not a ton of
variance ... what kind of levers we can pull to make it more fun."

## Why it reads as formulaic

A run today is the same three stops (Game, Rivalry, Rest) and the same three
rewards (cards, a player, maybe a perk). Every opponent is "a franchise at a
share of the cap", so a game against Utah plays like a game against Miami;
the only thing that changes run to run is what you draft. Slay the Spire's
variance mostly lives in the places the Climb has no stand-in for yet:

| Slay the Spire | The Climb today |
|---|---|
| ? rooms (events) | none |
| Merchant (spend gold) | none |
| Elites (hard, a relic each) | Rivalries, partly (a perk 70% of the time) |
| Enemies with their own moves | every coach plays its archetype's deck |
| Boss abilities | a classic team, no rule of its own |
| Potions (one-use, held between fights) | none |
| Neow's opening choice | three boons |
| Ascension | the coach rungs |

## The levers, best first

### 1. ? stops: events (StS's ? rooms) ★
A new map stop, about a fifth of floors 2-6. A short scene with two or three
choices, usually a trade-off. Every one is something a real team lives
through:
- **Trade call.** Swap your lowest-paid player for one of three better ones;
  the cap must still fit.
- **Locker-room dispute.** Your star sits the next game, or every starter
  carries 4 minutes into it.
- **Agent on the phone.** Pay part of the pot to raise your cap $600.
- **Film session.** Upgrade one card, or remove two.
- **Two-way contract.** A free cheap player, who leaves after three games.
- **National TV game.** The next game adds double to the pot; lose it and
  you lose a perk too.
- **Injury scare.** Gamble: 50% a player is out for the next game, 50% they
  come back with a hot marker.
- **Summer-league tape.** Take a random rare card, with a dead card ("Bad
  Contract": it does nothing) shuffled in beside it.

The single biggest lever: no two runs walk through the same scenes.

### 2. Opponent identities ★
Each franchise's coach plays a signature rule, shown on the map before you
choose the stop, so the route is a real decision: *Seven Seconds* (Phoenix:
every roll of theirs +1 in the first section), *Grit and Grind* (Memphis:
your paint checks −1), *Splash* (Golden State: their threes +1), *Bad Boys*
(Detroit: a foul-trouble card in their opening hand), *Lob City*
(Clippers), *Process* (Philadelphia: a bench that never tires). About one
rule per franchise; Act I uses the gentle ones.

### 3. Boss abilities ★
Each classic boss gets a rule of its own, announced in the scouting report:
the '96 Bulls' *Jordan Rules* (their best player gets a Clutch Possession
every section), *Showtime* (their fast-break cards cost nothing), the 2014
Spurs' *Beautiful Game* (every card they play draws one). Beating it is a
story, not a bigger cap.

### 4. Free Agency stops: spend the pot (StS's merchant)
Not a new currency: the pot's own coins, spent now instead of banked. Buy a
player, a card, a card removal, or a perk at a price. The tension is Slay's
gold: spend to get stronger, or carry it to the bank.

### 5. Locker-room items (StS's potions)
Two slots, one use each, found after wins and at events: *Ice Pack*
(clear one player's tracker), *Hype Video* (+1 to every roll for a section),
*Scouting Tape* (see the coach's hand for one section), *Timeout Bonus* (a
second timeout this game). Small swings you choose when to spend.

### 6. Elite stops (StS's elites)
A harder fight marked on the map (a "superteam": the franchise's best three
on a bigger cap) that always drops a perk and a rare. Rivalries could become
these, or sit beside them.

### 7. A richer opening (Neow)
Training Camp's boons with trade-offs: start with a star over the cap but
two cards fewer; start with two perks and a Bad Contract; a random playbook
with a bonus card.

### 8. Daily Climb
One seeded run a day, the same map and events for everyone, a leaderboard by
pot. Variance that you share with friends (the Friends feature already
exists).

### 9. Ascension-style modifiers
Optional stacked rules for a bigger pot (coaches open hot, no Rest Day before
the boss, a curse in the starting deck), on top of the coach rungs.

## My first batch

**? stops, opponent identities and boss abilities.** They change what a run
*is* rather than how big its numbers are: what you walk into, who you plan
around, and how each act ends. Free Agency and items come next. Every new
card or rule is run by the user before it ships, as always.
